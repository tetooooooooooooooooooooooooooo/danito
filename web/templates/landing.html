Why retention is the number that matters

Getting listed in Discovery isn't about how many people joined. It's about how many stayed. Discord looks at whether your members are still around weeks later, so a server with a thousand people who drift off does worse than a smaller one that keeps them.

An example of what /retention shows you. Real numbers start the day you add it.

  1. Someone joins. They quietly get a role named after that day, so everybody who arrived together forms one group.
  2. About a week later. That group gets a single ping in your ratings channel, right at the point people usually go quiet for good. It deletes itself two seconds afterwards, so they get the notification without the clutter.
  3. They come back and rate you. One tap, 1 to 10. Being pulled back into the server is the point; the score tells you whether they liked what they found.
  4. You watch the number move. {{ brand }} tracks how many of each joining group are still here after 1, 7, 14 and 30 days, so you can see your retention rather than guess at it.

Each member is nudged once, not weekly. A bot that pings your members repeatedly costs you the retention you were trying to build.
